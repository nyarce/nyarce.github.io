About

I've done a lot of different things, but I have a habit of falling into the coordinator role regardless. College house parties, I organized them. Bartending, I became the de-facto inventory manager. Waiting tables, I ended up as head expo during SXSW week. There's always a switchboard and I'm usually the one standing at it. I have a Trello board for household plans. I know.

I think good process is an act of care. When work is organized and communication is clear, people can actually do their best work without burning energy on confusion. That's what I'm trying to build every time I step into a project. I lead with trust and go from there.

I am also chaotically organized. My systems look like a mess from the outside: seventeen browser tabs open, a tracking doc only I can navigate, sticky notes that make total sense to me. Nothing falls through the cracks, though. The system doesn't have to look good to work.

Eight years managing capital projects in the Permian Basin confirmed this. $10M budgets, 2,000+ miles of pipeline, teams across engineering, operations, regulatory, and finance. All of it moving at once. The chaos isn't the problem. The chaos is the job. You build systems that hold, or things break. When I moved into software PM, the tools changed. Same instincts.

  • Finding the weird problem before it becomes the real one
  • Building the process before someone asks for it
  • Figuring it out, and bringing people with me
  • Not stopping until it's something worth being proud of

Work that matters to real people, with a team that actually gives a damn. Mission-driven orgs in climate, water resources, or public infrastructure are where I'm looking. I want to be somewhere the people I work with and the people we serve are both part of the picture.

How I Work

I've done enough self-reflection (and one formal assessment) to know what actually makes me effective, and what I need to do my best work. Here's the honest version.

The honest truth

I am chaotically organized

My systems look like a mess from the outside. Seventeen browser tabs, a tracking doc only I can navigate, sticky notes that make total sense to me. Nothing falls through the cracks, though. I've just stopped pretending the system is going to look pretty while I'm in it.

What I actually enjoy

The weird problem, every time

Give me the thing nobody has a playbook for. The process that's been quietly wrong for two years, the edge case that keeps getting kicked down the road. I like figuring those out. Not as a coping mechanism, just genuinely.

How I work with people

I figure it out and bring people with me

Part of the job is making sure everyone understands the path and actually wants to take it. I don't get far ahead of the team. Trust, context, and momentum are the conditions I'm always trying to build.

How I show up

I lead with trust and build from there

My default is to assume good intent. People know their domain, they want to do good work, and when something's off it's usually a communication problem not a people problem. Starting from that assumption tends to make the work go better for everyone.

What you can count on

I won't ship something I'm not proud of

If quality is getting cut for speed, I'll say something. I'll come with data and do it early enough to actually matter. I care about whether the work is good, not just whether it crossed the finish line.

What gets me going

Watching something broken become clean

When a process that didn't work starts working, or a team that was struggling clicks into place, that part of the job genuinely doesn't get old. A release that just goes smoothly. That's what I'm in it for.

I C

Took a DISC Strengths assessment recently. Scored as The Performer (Influence and Compliance), apparently the most creative temperament blend in the model. People-driven and quality-obsessed in equal measure. Honestly? That tracks. I'll take it.
